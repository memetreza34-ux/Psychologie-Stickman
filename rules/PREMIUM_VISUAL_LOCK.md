## What premium quality means

Every final frame should feel like a still from a polished adult psychology documentary / editorial graphic-novel animation.

Quality comes from:
- consistent `STYLIZED_STICKMAN_CAST_V2` anatomy
- mature non-chibi character proportions
- expressive body language and role clarity
- believable scene-specific environments
- purposeful camera angle
- soft cel shading with stronger graphic shadow when appropriate
- motivated cinematic illustrated light
- foreground/midground/background separation when useful
- controlled texture
- small story-relevant details
- strong focal hierarchy
- mood that matches the current spoken beat
- visible power relation when the beat depends on hierarchy or influence

Quality does not mean photorealism, 3D, excessive detail or normal human anatomy.

## Premium environment — HARD LOCK

The simple figures are supported by a richer illustrated world.

For normal real-life scenes, prefer:
- recognizable architecture
- a few concrete topic-relevant props
- foreground/midground/background
- intentional depth and overlap
- motivated practical/window/screen light
- a clear focal subject against a quieter environment

Avoid as defaults:
- empty cream/off-white background
- one centered character floating in blank space
- repeated generic classroom/office backgrounds
- sterile flat rooms
- stock-vector staging
- repeated straight-on lineup compositions

Sparse negative space is allowed only when it serves the beat, for example isolation, clarity or a deliberate concept frame.

## Premium composition — HARD LOCK

Use composition to tell the psychological idea:
- dominant focal point
- clear silhouette
- asymmetry when useful
- foreground occlusion/depth when useful
- camera distance chosen for the beat
- visible social spacing and hierarchy
- no unnecessary visual clutter

Do not default to a centered character plus evenly spaced background figures.

A `SPLIT`, `CONTRAST` or `MONTAGE` composition is allowed only for one scene's single beat. Never use it to combine several different scene numbers into one asset; see `rules/FLOW_OUTPUT_MODE.md`.

## Adaptive mood quality

Every scene should visually honor its planned mood and power relation.

Examples:
- `CLEAR`: open readable space and natural color.
- `UNEASY`: slightly tighter framing, directional light, subtle unease.
- `DARK`: motivated low-key light, shadows, depth and tension — not empty blackness.
- `POWER`: hierarchy through framing, blocking, scale and posture.
- `MONEY`: status/financial cues through environment, props, graphite/green/gold possibilities without finance-stock-art clichés.
- `ISOLATION`: negative space, distance, cool sparse environment.
- `HOPE`: opening composition, clearer warm light, visual relief.

## Character quality

Premium characters are defined by:
- strict mature stickman anatomy
- readable silhouette
- role-specific simplified clothing
- meaningful pose/action
- restrained but effective facial expression
- scene-appropriate prop only when useful
- relation to other characters and environment

Do not make all characters friendly or polished. A manipulator may look controlled, a target withdrawn, an authority figure imposing, or an exhausted person visibly worn down while preserving the same anatomy family.

Never use cute/chibi/mascot appeal as a shortcut for polish.

## Color quality

Use controlled color with recurring channel language, but do not tint every scene the same way.

Useful recurring anchors may include deep navy, graphite, muted teal, burgundy, coral, amber, deep green, restrained gold and warm neutral.

Hard rules:
- no candy-color default
- no grey/beige/cream wash across the whole video
- strongest useful contrast belongs to the focal subject/relation
- local object colors remain believable
- mood may change temperature and saturation

## World consistency

Character scenes, schemas, object metaphors, comparisons and text illustrations share:
- same outline language
- same 2D rendering
- same cel-shading family
- same texture logic
- same mature editorial tone
- same overall polish

Lighting and color may change strongly with mood; rendering style may not.

## Priority

Prefer the representation that communicates the beat fastest:
1. concrete human action/relationship when behavior is central
2. environment/object metaphor when it communicates faster
3. schema when mechanism genuinely needs structure

Do not force a real-life character scene when abstraction is clearer, and do not force a diagram when human dynamics are the actual point.

## QA

Regenerate if:
- mood does not match the spoken beat
- dark scene is only a black background
- power relation is semantically important but visually flat
- every character looks pleasant regardless of role
- character looks cute/chibi/mascot-like
- torso/limbs drift toward normal cartoon-human anatomy
- frame defaults to empty cream/off-white space without semantic reason
- environment feels generic or sterile when a real location is needed
- composition looks like a storyboard panel sheet or contact sheet
- scene relies on cheap evil clichés
- frame drifts toward bare stickman doodle, normal cartoon human, 3D/CGI, corporate vector or photorealism
- representation does not explain the beat

`rules/VISUAL_SYSTEM.md` is authoritative.
