# REPRESENTATION VARIETY LOCK — FUTURE YOUTUBE VIDEOS

**STATUS: BINDING FOR NEW/FUTURE YOUTUBE PROJECTS**

Policy ID: `REPRESENTATION_VARIETY_V1`

Existing/historical projects are not retroactively migrated unless the user explicitly requests it.

## Core rule

Do not force every visual beat into the same character-scene pattern.

For every spoken beat, choose the clearest visual form first. A strong video should feel visually varied while still belonging to one coherent channel world.

Allowed primary forms:
- `CHARACTER_SCENE`
- `SCHEMA_EXPLAINER`
- `OBJECT_METAPHOR`
- `COMPARE_VISUAL`
- `TEXT_ILLUSTRATION`
- environment/object-only illustration when no person is needed

## Character presence

Humans are optional.

Use a character scene when body language, action, reaction, hierarchy, social pressure or relationship is central.

Prefer a no-human visual when an object, environment, comparison or mechanism explains the beat faster.

Never add a Stickman only to fill empty space.

Normal longform guidance:
- `CHARACTER_SCENE`: about 45–60%
- `SCHEMA_EXPLAINER`: about 10–20%
- `OBJECT_METAPHOR`: about 10–20%
- `COMPARE_VISUAL`: about 10–20%
- `TEXT_ILLUSTRATION`: normally 0–10%

These are guidance ranges, not quotas. Clarity of the current beat wins.

## Text restraint — HARD LOCK

Visible text is not decoration.

Use text only when it materially improves understanding, for example:
- cover/title
- exact psychology term
- important number or price
- short comparison label
- short schema label
- one memorable concept phrase

Do not put visible text on every image.
Do not repeat the spoken sentence as on-screen text.
If text is not necessary: `ZERO READABLE TEXT`.

## Schema restraint — HARD LOCK

Schemas are used only when the viewer benefits from seeing a mechanism, sequence, relation or comparison structurally.

Do not use a schema merely because the beat is abstract.
Do not create long runs of diagram-like frames.
Schemas must remain cinematic illustrations in the same channel world, never PowerPoint or corporate-vector slides.

## Color and mood variety — HARD LOCK

One coherent world does NOT mean one repeated color treatment.

Do not default the whole video to the same red/burgundy/dark treatment, the same teal/cream treatment or any single accent palette.

Color, light and mood must follow the current beat.
Use the available mood range deliberately: `CLEAR`, `UNEASY`, `DARK`, `POWER`, `MONEY`, `ISOLATION`, `HOPE`.

A difficult topic may still contain neutral, clear or hopeful frames. A neutral topic does not need forced darkness.

Across the video, vary when useful:
- warm vs cool light
- day vs evening/night
- bright vs low-key exposure
- local environment colors
- accent colors
- camera distance and angle
- human vs no-human representation

Keep illustration identity fixed while scene mood and palette adapt.

## Sequence-level variety

Avoid repetitive runs such as:
- several consecutive medium shots of one Stickman in similar rooms
- several consecutive red/dark frames without narrative reason
- several consecutive text cards
- several consecutive schemas
- the same centered composition over and over

When adjacent beats allow it, vary representation, camera, environment or mood while preserving continuity.

## QA hard fail

Review/regenerate the plan if:
- nearly every scene contains a character without a clear reason
- text appears on most frames without explanatory need
- schema/text frames are used as filler
- the same red/burgundy/dark or teal/cream treatment dominates mechanically across the whole video
- consecutive scenes repeatedly use the same composition despite different beats
- representation variety breaks the unified visual world
