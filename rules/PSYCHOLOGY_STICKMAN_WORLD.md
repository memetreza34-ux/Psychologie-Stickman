**STATUS: ACTIVE / USER-APPROVED**

Policy ID: `PSYCHOLOGY_STICKMAN_WORLD_V1`

This is the binding visual world for new psychology / human-behavior YouTube productions.

## 1. Core look

### Shared channel core, individual episode direction

All episodes keep the same professional illustrator language: clean expressive ink contours with a slight organic variation, clear foreground silhouettes, quieter background detail, soft two-/three-value cel shading and restrained fine paper grain. Avoid glossy 3D volume, noisy sketch lines, heavy watercolor washes, blurry pseudo-cinematic backgrounds and detached vector infographics. Materials, hands, props, rooms and explanatory diagrams receive the same drawing treatment as the people.

Each episode develops a small set of compatible color/material accents, an appropriate lighting treatment, locations and individually designed people from its script. This is art direction within the common world, not permission to invent a new illustration style for each video. Palette accents are not a global color filter; natural local colors and meaningful warm/cool changes remain possible.

After explicit final cover approval, the approved `thumbnail.png` is the mandatory visual style reference for EVERY generated scene, including objects, rooms, diagrams, comparisons and text illustrations. Character references supplement it only when the particular identifiable recurring person is visible. Do not introduce a person merely to use their reference.

Create a high-quality hand-drawn 2D animated story world. Every frame should feel as if it was drawn by the same professional illustrator for the same channel:
- strong slightly organic dark outlines
- soft cel shading
- subtle illustrated texture
- natural clear colors
- clean readable silhouettes
- believable foreground / middle ground / background depth when the scene is spatial
- lived-in environments instead of empty clip-art

No fixed channel palette. Color and light follow the scene.

## 2. The world is bigger than the cast

A beat may be represented by:
- `CHARACTER_SCENE`
- `OBJECT_FOCUS`
- `ENVIRONMENT_SCENE`
- `SCHEMA_EXPLAINER`
- `COMPARE_VISUAL`
- `TEXT_ILLUSTRATION`

Humans are optional. Use a full character scene only when visible human behavior, emotion, hierarchy, interaction or social relation is the clearest evidence.

All non-human frames still use the same illustrator language: outlines, cel shading, texture, color handling and professional finish.

## 3. Stickman species — HARD DNA whenever people appear

All visible people belong to exactly one visual species:
- oversized round or softly oval light/white head
- tiny black oval eyes
- minimal brows and mouth only as needed
- narrow simplified clothed torso
- very thin dark stick-like arms and legs
- small simplified hands and shoes
- clothing follows the thin stickman construction and must not create realistic human body volume

**Hard fail:** a person may never drift into a normal cartoon human, semi-realistic human, chibi person or full-bodied animated character.

Hair, age, clothing, profession and historical era may change. The underlying anatomy may not.

### Same-frame species lock

When two or more people are visible together, they must all visibly share the same base construction:
- same light/white head shell and head proportions
- same eye language
- same narrow torso scale
- same very thin limb thickness
- same simplified hand/shoe construction

Hair is layered onto the approved light/white head; it must not create a skin-toned normal-human face. Clothing is layered onto the narrow torso; it must not create realistic shoulders, chest, hips or thighs.

Never accept a mixed frame where one figure is a normal cartoon person while another is an approved stickman. **Mixed species in one frame = regenerate.**

For every human-containing BILD prompt, reinforce the compact species cue when useful:
`same approved stickman species: identical light round/oval base heads, tiny black oval eyes, narrow clothed torsos, very thin dark stick-like limbs; hair/clothing may vary but no normal-human anatomy.`

If any generated human frame breaks the species, regenerate that frame instead of accepting the drift.

## 4. Individual people

There is no permanent channel mascot and no mandatory Character A/B cast. Design each person for the current role through hairstyle, clothing, accessories, age cues, profession/social role, era, posture and expression.

Reuse an exact identity only when the story clearly follows the same person.

## 5. Emotion and dark psychology

Show psychology through observable evidence. With people, use gaze, head angle, shoulders, hands, distance, standing/sitting, blocking, group spacing and object interaction.

Dark psychology may be tense, intimate, cold or shadowed, but avoid horror shortcuts such as demon eyes, horns or monster cues. A dark beat also does not automatically require two people; a phone, doorway, message, empty chair arrangement or object-led frame may work better.

## 6. Adaptive worlds

The drawing/render language stays fixed while the world changes with the script: apartment, school, office, café, store, station, laboratory, hospital, historical setting, workplace, street or bedroom. Architecture, furniture, props, clothing and hair should fit place and era.

## 7. Color and lighting

Use natural varied colors. Do not mechanically repeat one beige/cream explainer background, teal tint or any other single palette across the whole video.

Neutral explanatory scenes do **not** default to a flat beige presentation board. When the beat can live in a real place or object context, prefer a believable illustrated setting, shelf, table, room, product close-up or environmental composition.

## 8. Visual staging + motif progression — HARD REVIEW

Representation variety is not enough if every frame still looks like the same flat explainer card or repeats the same hero object in nearly the same role.

Across the sequence vary, when supported by content:
- spatial depth: immersive room/environment vs close object vs clean graphic frame
- camera: wide / medium / close / top-down / over-shoulder when useful
- subject scale and placement
- foreground / middle / background usage
- local lighting and palette
- negative space and focal density
- recurring object state, action and consequence

A recurring story object may remain for continuity, but each nearby recurrence should visibly progress the story. Example: `store choice -> receipt/bag aftermath -> top-down selection -> macro feature inspection -> broken hinge` is progression; repeated near-identical product-display shots are not.

Review/rewrite when several adjacent images repeat the same beige/neutral board, centered-object layout, circles/arrows template, near-identical camera setup or effectively the same motif role.

## 9. Objects, schemas and text

Objects and environments may carry a frame without people.

Schemas must stay simple enough to read instantly on a phone:
- one mechanism
- preferably no more than 3 major labels/branches
- default maximum 2 distinct visible states/panels
- no crowd of tiny figures
- no dense web of arrows, icons and mini-panels
- no 3-/4-part mini-storyboard inside one image as a normal solution

If a mechanism needs three steps, split it across BILDs, show one coherent physical scene, or reduce it to a two-state comparison.

Text-led frames may use exact planned German text when wording itself matters. Keep it large and sparse. No paragraph-like copy or tiny explanatory text. If no text is planned: `NO VISIBLE TEXT`.

Any unplanned readable English, German, UI copy, packaging copy or gibberish is a hard fail and requires regeneration.

## 10. Mobile readability

Every frame must still work when viewed small on a phone.

Hard review when:
- the main point depends on tiny labels
- more than a few small figures compete for attention
- several arrows/circles/mini-panels create clutter
- text is too small to read quickly
- the focal object/person is too small relative to the frame
- more than 2 separate states/panels compete for attention

## 11. Cover and episode reference

First generate only the necessary individual character-reference assets. Finish that stage before making exactly three separate cover candidates. Then WAIT while the user chooses, revises and finishes the cover. A cover selection alone does not authorize scene generation. Proceed only after the user explicitly approves the final cover AND authorizes starting scenes; a natural-language instruction can do both at once.

- Final winner becomes `05-export/thumbnail.png`, separate from `03-bilder/01.png`.
- Attach/select that same approved cover as style reference for EVERY image, including scenes without people and all diagrams. Never fall back to an unselected candidate or a new style.
- Use it for linework, shading, grain, materials, color relationships and overall finish. Do not copy its title, layout, pose, depicted people or story content into unrelated scenes.
- Add only the character references needed for identifiable recurring people actually visible in that scene. Object/diagram-only images normally have no character reference.
- Each BILD explicitly lists its mandatory cover style reference and its character-reference set, including `none` when appropriate.
- Once final approval releases the scenes, run all five-image batches without further approval questions. Regenerate failures against the same approved cover.
- If the interface cannot actually reuse the cover reference, report that precise limitation instead of claiming consistent reference use.

## 12. Short style anchor

`Same approved Psychology Stickman World: high-quality hand-drawn 2D animation with organic dark outlines, soft cel shading, subtle illustration texture, natural clear colors and coherent depth. When people appear they use the exact same stickman species: identical oversized round/soft-oval light heads, tiny black oval eyes, narrow clothed torsos and very thin dark stick-like limbs; hair/clothing may vary but never normal cartoon-human anatomy. Objects, text, environments and schemas use the same illustrator language.`

## 13. Minimal exclusions

- no 3D / Pixar
- no anime
- no photorealism
- no normal-human anatomy drift
- no mixed stickman + normal-cartoon-human frame
- no bare whiteboard doodle
- no foreign corporate infographic style
- no repetitive beige explainer-board default
- no 3-/4-panel explainer-board default
- no unplanned readable text

## 14. QA hard fail / review

Reject/regenerate when:
- any person stops looking like the approved stickman species
- two people in the same frame use different base human species/anatomies
- hair replaces the approved white/light head with a normal human face
- clothing creates realistic shoulders, hips, thighs or normal cartoon-human volume
- figures become blank mannequins
- every beat becomes a character scene by default
- a person is added only to fill space
- object/text/schema frames look like another illustrator
- several adjacent frames repeat the same flat explainer-board layout
- several adjacent frames reuse the same main object without meaningful visual progression
- schemas rely on 3+ panels/stages, tiny figures or cluttered arrows/labels
- text is not comfortably readable on a phone
- any unplanned readable text appears
- environments look photorealistic or stylistically detached
- later frames look like unrelated illustrators

## 15. Core sentence

**Same illustrated world and exact same stickman species whenever people appear; representation, people, clothing, place, era, camera, color, light, motif state and emotion adapt to the script without mixed anatomy, repetitive object staging or overloaded explainer boards.**
