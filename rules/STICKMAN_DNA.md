# STICKMAN DNA — STYLIZED HUMAN SOURCE OF TRUTH v2

**STATUS: HARD LOCK — CINEMATIC HUMAN BEHAVIOR WORLD v2**

The full world is defined in `rules/VISUAL_SYSTEM.md`. This file defines human character anatomy, emotional range and role expression.

Character system: `STYLIZED_STICKMAN_CAST_V2`

Anatomy sub-policy: `GEOMETRIC_STICKMAN_ANATOMY_V1`

## 1. Unified human cast — absolute rule

Every visible human or human-like person belongs to the exact same stylized strict-stickman anatomy family.

This applies to main characters, supporting characters, background people, crowds, researchers, authority figures, reflections, screens, photos and memory inserts.

**Recognition test:** if a figure could reasonably be described as a thin normal cartoon human, the anatomy is WRONG. It must unmistakably read as an illustrated stick figure at first glance.

## 2. Geometric anatomy lock — HIGHEST CHARACTER PRIORITY

Every visible person uses the following geometry.

### HEAD
- one clean simple circle or slightly vertical oval
- head diameter roughly similar to the visible torso height, never a baby/chibi giant head
- no realistic skull/jaw construction
- no ears
- no nose
- no neck

### FACE
- two tiny dot eyes or very short eye marks
- one tiny simple mouth line
- optional tiny eyebrow strokes only
- no cheeks, eyelids, detailed lips, nostrils or realistic face construction

### TORSO
- one very small narrow graphic clothing shape: narrow rounded rectangle or very simple tapered block
- torso width stays clearly below half the head width
- no chest volume
- no waist shaping
- no hips
- no broad shoulder mass
- no anatomical ribcage silhouette

### ARMS
- ultra-thin line-like illustrated limbs with near-uniform thickness
- visually read as stick-figure strokes, not skinny human arms
- no upper-arm mass
- no forearm mass
- no biceps
- no sleeve volume that makes the arm human-thick

### LEGS
- ultra-thin line-like illustrated limbs with near-uniform thickness
- visually read as stick-figure strokes, not skinny human legs
- no thighs
- no calves
- no filled trouser-leg volume
- no hip-to-thigh anatomy

### HANDS
- tiny dot/mitten/minimal endpoint shapes only
- no fingers
- no palm anatomy

### FEET
- small flat simple shoe shapes only
- shoes must not become large mascot feet

### CLOTHING
Clothing is a **flat graphic cue**, not a human body builder.

- torso garment wraps only the tiny narrow torso
- sleeves may add color along a line arm but may NOT widen it into a normal cartoon sleeve
- trousers may color the line legs but may NOT become filled human trouser legs
- jackets, hoodies, shirts and coats must not create chest, shoulder, waist, hip, thigh or calf volume

### HAIR
- one tiny flat graphic hair cue attached to the head edge
- no volumetric hairstyle
- no realistic hair mass

## 3. Line-limb thickness lock — NON-NEGOTIABLE

Arms and legs must remain visually much thinner than the head outline and torso width.

They should look like clean illustrated strokes with a little intentional line weight, not cylinders or tapered human limbs.

Hard fail examples:
- elbow-to-wrist volume
- visible thigh/calf shape
- sleeve-shaped arm
- trouser-shaped leg
- normal cartoon shoulder-to-arm transition

## 4. Mature proportion lock — NON-NEGOTIABLE

The channel uses adult editorial stick figures, not cute mascots.

Reject:
- chibi proportions
- baby-like oversized heads
- short rounded plush arms/legs
- broad shoulders
- large soft hoodie bodies
- thick calves/forearms
- toy-like feet or hands
- blush/cute facial styling
- children's-book posing

Mature does NOT mean adding realistic anatomy. Maturity comes from posture, camera, clothing cues, environment and expression while the body remains unmistakably stick-figure geometry.

## 5. Emotional and moral range

The character family must support the full human-behavior channel.

Allowed presentation includes relaxed, confident, curious, skeptical, insecure, anxious, ashamed, exhausted, sad, isolated, defensive, suspicious, dominant, intimidating, cold, arrogant, controlling, deceptive-friendly, manipulative, authoritative and vulnerable.

Emotion is expressed mainly through:
- head angle
- tiny eye/mouth changes
- line-arm position
- line-leg stance
- torso lean
- distance from others
- gaze direction
- blocking and framing

Do not create human body mass to make emotion more readable.

## 6. Psychological role expression

### Neutral / everyday
Open line-limb posture, normal gaze, natural spacing, simple clothing cues.

### Vulnerable / target
Withdrawn head/torso angle, arms closer to torso, edge placement, looking away or back, more negative space when useful.

### Dominant / authority
Stable upright line posture, deliberate stillness, central/foreground placement, lower camera angle when useful. Never add broad shoulders or muscular mass.

### Manipulator / deceptive figure
Controlled posture, calm minimal face, selective tiny smile, strategic proximity or positioning. No monster styling.

### Isolated / excluded
Separated from group, edge/distant placement, reduced visual support, colder or emptier surrounding space.

### Crowd / group pressure
Every background person uses the same exact geometric stickman family. Pressure comes from repeated direction, clustering, gaze and spacing — never from switching background people into normal cartoon silhouettes.

## 7. Villain-cliché prohibition

Do not default to glowing red eyes, horns, monster teeth, demonic faces, exaggerated evil grin or supernatural shadow monster unless the literal subject requires it.

Psychological threat should usually come from posture, gaze, proximity, hierarchy, framing, shadow, stillness, environmental control and another person's reaction.

## 8. Individuality without anatomy drift

Individualize through:
- tiny flat torso garment shape/color
- tiny hair mark
- glasses
- one simple accessory
- modest height variation
- minimal face variation
- posture
- silhouette

Avoid fashion-rendered clothing. Identity must not depend on realistic sleeves, trousers, shoulders or hair volume.

## 9. Background people — HARD LOCK

Background figures are a common failure point and must NOT be downgraded into normal cartoon people.

Every background/crowd figure keeps:
- same simple circle/oval head family
- tiny narrow torso shape
- ultra-thin line arms and legs
- no human shoulder/chest/hip volume
- no thick seated-human silhouettes

Allowed simplification: fewer facial marks, fewer accessories and less shading.

Not allowed: chunky/cute bodies, realistic seated silhouettes, generic corporate humans or different anatomy families.

## 10. Human presence is optional

Not every frame needs a character. Prefer a no-human representation when a schema, object, environment, comparison or metaphor explains the beat more clearly. Never insert a person just to fill empty space.

## 11. Continuity

If the same person returns, preserve head shape, tiny hair/accessory cue, torso-garment color, line-limb proportions, shoes and face simplification. Mood, pose and light may change.

## 12. Character-reference approval gate

A generated reference is NOT authoritative automatically.

Approve only if:
- it unmistakably reads as a true illustrated stick figure
- torso width remains clearly below half the head width
- arms and legs read as ultra-thin strokes, not skinny human limbs
- clothing does not create sleeves/trouser legs with normal human volume
- there is no neck, chest, waist, hip, thigh or calf anatomy
- head remains mature and non-chibi
- background/reference rendering remains polished and editorial

Reject immediately if the silhouette could pass for a thin cartoon human.

## 13. Hard fail

Regenerate if any visible human becomes:
- bare black-line whiteboard doodle
- thin normal cartoon human
- full cartoon-human anatomy
- semi-realistic or realistic human
- generic corporate person
- cute/chibi/mascot character
- broad-shouldered body
- thick or tapered human limbs
- sleeve-volume arms
- trouser-volume legs
- chest/waist/hip anatomy
- detailed hands/fingers
- realistic face anatomy
- 3D/Pixar/CGI
- anime/manga/chibi
- another character family
