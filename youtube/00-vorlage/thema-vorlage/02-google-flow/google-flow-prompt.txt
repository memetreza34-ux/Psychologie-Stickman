Normal scene: usually ONE dominant accent color plus background and ink. Use a second accent only when comparison or cause/effect genuinely requires it. Do not invent a new project palette.

SPARSE ENVIRONMENT — HARD LOCK
Suggest the place; do not fully render it.
- normally use only 1–5 relevant environmental props
- large clean negative space
- flat shapes
- simple floor/wall line only when useful
- little or no texture
- little or no shading
- only enough perspective to understand the action

Examples:
- cinema = 2–4 seats + screen/door hint + ticket
- café = table + chairs + cup
- office = desk + laptop + chair
- train = seat/window/door/handrail hints
- home = one furniture cue + relevant object

NO CINEMATIC LIGHTING.
NO RICH DETAILED ENVIRONMENTS.
NO MATERIAL TEXTURES.
NO COMPLEX CEL SHADING.
NO DECORATION WITHOUT EXPLANATORY VALUE.

ONE CLEAR VISUAL IDEA — HARD LOCK
The viewer should understand the current spoken beat in about one second.
Use:
- one dominant subject/action
- at most 1–2 supporting clues
- strong silhouette
- large negative space
- simple visual hierarchy

Never add detail just to make the image feel expensive.

SCENE FAMILIES
Choose the simplest family that explains the beat:
- EVERYDAY_MINIMAL — concrete daily-life action with minimal place cues
- CONCEPT_METAPHOR — simple psychology metaphor for invisible/internal mechanisms
- COMPARE_CHANGE — clean A/B, before/after, stay/leave, problem/solution comparison
- DIAGRAM — rare last resort only

Planning target for a normal psychology video:
- about 60% EVERYDAY_MINIMAL
- about 25% CONCEPT_METAPHOR
- about 10% COMPARE_CHANGE
- at most about 5% DIAGRAM

VISUAL VARIETY — HARD LOCK
Vary pose, framing, cast and scene family when the spoken content benefits.
Do not create variation by adding environmental detail.
Do not default to one-stickman-standing-in-center.
Split/contrast is allowed when both sides explain the SAME spoken beat.
Montage is allowed only when all parts explain the SAME spoken beat and remain visually simple.

SOCIAL SCENE VARIETY — HARD LOCK
Use duo/group scenes when the beat is actually social: conversation, feedback, observation, family, friends, coworkers, group influence, experiments or comparison.
Do not add extra people to internal solo beats.
